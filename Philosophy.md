# Philosophy

> "This document is intentionally unfinished. It should evolve as I do."

Last Updated: August 2026

---

# Why This Exists

This is not a mission statement.

It is not a personal brand exercise.

It is not something written to impress other people.

This document exists to remind me what I currently believe, why I am building the life I am building, and to provide a compass whenever I feel lost or distracted.

I expect many of these ideas to evolve.

That is the point.

---

# My Current Understanding

I do not believe I have discovered my life's purpose.

I am still searching for it.

But I have realized something important:

If I cannot yet answer why I exist, I can still choose how I want to exist.

For now, my answer is simple:

I want my existence to add more value than it consumes.

I want to leave humanity slightly better than I found it.

---

# My North Star

I want to spend my life increasing humanity's ability to flourish.

Not because I believe I can change the world alone.

But because I believe every meaningful contribution becomes part of humanity's collective progress.

Medicine, engineering, programming, entrepreneurship, research, and future projects are not my identity.

They are simply different languages for contributing.

The destination is not becoming a doctor.

The destination is becoming genuinely useful.

---

# Why Medicine

Medicine was not necessarily my childhood calling.

Growing up in India, education carried the hopes of my family.

After my first unsuccessful attempt at medical entrance exams, I discovered the IMAT examination and chose a different path.

Looking back, that decision became one of the first major decisions that truly felt like my own.

During these years studying medicine, I have come to appreciate something deeper than the profession itself.

Medicine teaches me to understand people before problems.

It teaches responsibility.

It teaches humility.

It teaches that every statistic is ultimately a human being.

Medicine gave me a language for understanding humanity.

---

# Why Engineering

Engineering represents humanity's ability to create.

Every bridge, satellite, medical device, algorithm, and spacecraft exists because someone imagined a better future and decided to build it.

I admire that spirit deeply.

I want to spend my life creating things that are genuinely useful.

Not simply because they are technically impressive, but because they improve people's lives.

Engineering teaches me how ideas become reality.

---

# Why Programming

Programming fascinates me because it transforms ideas into working systems.

It allows imagination to become tangible.

Learning to program is learning another language for creation.

Code is not the destination.

It is a tool that allows ideas to exist outside my own mind.

---

# Why I Care About Space

Space is not simply about rockets.

It represents humanity's long-term future.

I am inspired by the idea that humanity continues exploring, learning, and expanding beyond today's limitations.

Whether or not I ever work directly in space-related fields is secondary.

What matters is contributing to humanity's long-term ability to survive, grow, and continue asking bigger questions.

---

# What I Believe

Knowledge matters most when it eventually helps someone.

Curiosity should lead to creation.

Creation should lead to contribution.

Technology should make humanity more capable.

Medicine should make humanity healthier.

Engineering should make humanity stronger.

Science should make humanity wiser.

Progress should make humanity more compassionate as well as more capable.

---

# Principles I Want To Live By

Consume Less.
Create More.

Stay Curious.

Build Things.

Learn Continuously.

Question Assumptions.

Seek Truth Over Comfort.

Leave Every System Better Than You Found It.

Respect Every Person.

Think Long Term.

Be Useful.

Choose Contribution Over Recognition.

Create More Than You Consume.

---

# What Success Means

Success is not fame.

Success is not wealth alone.

Success is not titles.

Financial independence matters because it creates freedom.

But freedom is valuable only if it allows me to contribute more effectively.

When I am eighty years old, I hope success means that I helped build things that genuinely improved people's lives.

---

# The Kind Of Problems I Want To Work On

I am drawn toward problems that sit between disciplines.

Healthcare.

Biomedical engineering.

Artificial intelligence.

Programming.

Education.

Research.

Entrepreneurship.

Human flourishing.

Long-term civilization.

These interests are connected because meaningful human problems rarely fit inside a single field.

# What I Never Want To Become

Someone who stops learning.

Someone who builds only for money.

Someone who values status over substance.

Someone who consumes endlessly without creating.

Someone who becomes cynical.

Someone who forgets why they started.

---

# The Person I Hope To Become

I hope people describe me as someone who was thoughtful.

Curious.

Kind.

Competent.

Reliable.

Creative.

Disciplined.

Someone who quietly built useful things.

Someone who left every place, every project, and every person slightly better than before.

What am I optimizing my life for?

What have I created during the last six months?

What have I learned?

Have my actions matched my stated values?

Am I consuming too much and creating too little?

What kind of person am I becoming?

What assumptions have changed?

# Closing Reflection

I may never fully answer why I exist.

Perhaps no one ever does.

But while I am alive, I can choose to learn.

I can choose to build.

I can choose to care.

I can choose to contribute.

If, years from now, the sum of those choices has helped even a small part of humanity move forward, then I will consider this life well spent.
